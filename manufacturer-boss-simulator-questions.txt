MANUFACTURER OWNER SIMULATOR
Questions and Options
=====================

QUESTION 1 — PRICE SIGNAL
Type: Single choice
Time limit: 40 seconds
Question value: 15 points

Question:
A key account wants the old price. Do you sign?

Scenario:
The market price of your core material has jumped 60%, while the weighted-average (WA) unit cost has risen only 22%. The customer wants the old price locked in for three months.

Live signal:
The input price is moving faster than cost can flow through opening WIP.

Options:
A. Sign. The reported cost still supports the current margin.
B. Raise the selling price by the full 60% immediately.
C. Reconcile WA with current replacement cost, then test the market ceiling and pass-through room.
D. Switch to FIFO and treat FIFO as a forward-looking cost.


QUESTION 2 — VOLUME RESPONSE
Type: Single choice
Time limit: 40 seconds
Question value: 20 points

Question:
Input prices are falling. Do you cut output too?

Scenario:
Spot material prices have fallen for several weeks, but opening WIP keeps the reported unit cost high. Sales says the demand dip may be temporary.

Live signal:
A stale high cost can make a profitable product look uncompetitive.

Options:
A. Cut every production line to protect the reported margin.
B. Hold the old plan until the reported cost catches up.
C. Refresh run-rate contribution and demand scenarios, then adjust volume by product.
D. Discount every product to buy back volume and ignore cost.


QUESTION 3 — CONSTRAINED MIX
Type: Single choice
Time limit: 50 seconds
Question value: 25 points

Question:
Only one product gets the bottleneck hours.

Scenario:
Core SKU shows a $100 reported unit margin and needs 4 bottleneck hours. Light SKU shows $55 and needs 2 hours. At replacement cost, their contributions are $80 and $70.

Live signal:
Core: $80 ÷ 4h = $20/h · Light: $70 ÷ 2h = $35/h

Options:
A. Prioritise Core because its unit margin is higher.
B. Prioritise Light because contribution per bottleneck hour is higher.
C. Split the hours evenly to avoid making the wrong call.
D. Prioritise whichever product sells the most units.


QUESTION 4 — GOVERNANCE DESIGN
Type: Multiple choice
Time limit: 60 seconds
Question value: 40 points
Instruction: Choose every valid rule. Incorrect selections deduct points.

Question:
Final call: which management rules do you approve?

Scenario:
WA remains the adopted financial-reporting method. You now need a weekly operating protocol that can withstand input-price volatility.

Live signal:
Select every rule you should approve. Incorrect selections deduct points.

Options:
A. Keep the adopted WA method for reporting, with consistency, reconciliation and an audit trail.
B. Use current replacement cost for short-term pricing, with a market ceiling and pass-through test.
C. Use incremental relevant cost and contribution per scarce resource for constrained product mix.
D. Bridge reported cost to economic cost in reviews, separating timing from operating performance.
E. Use one unit cost for every decision to eliminate debate over cost lenses.
F. Switch accounting methods whenever input prices move materially.
